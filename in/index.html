How the score works

How the Trust Index works. Each scan presents 10 questions from a larger pool, covering communication, emotional closeness, behaviour and trust.

Answers use a 1–5 scale. Negatively phrased questions are reverse-scored. The four area scores are converted to 0–100 and averaged to form your Trust Index.

These topics appear in relationship research, but RelationshipScan has not been clinically or psychometrically validated. The index reflects your answers at this moment. It cannot determine another person's intentions or predict the future of a relationship.

FAQ

12 clear answers before you decide.

What is RelationshipScan?

A 10-question self-reflection questionnaire about your experience in a relationship. The Trust Index is free; an extended report is optional.

How does the scan work?

You answer questions about recent experiences. The tool calculates four area scores and an overall index.

How accurate is the result?

The result reflects your answers. It is not clinically validated and cannot tell you what someone else is thinking.

Is this a psychological diagnosis?

No. It is an educational tool for reflection, not therapy or psychological, medical or legal advice.

What do I get for free?

Your Trust Index, a short interpretation and suggestions for reflection.

What is included in the full report?

A breakdown of four areas, response patterns and prompts for thought. Future scenarios are possibilities, not predictions.

Why do I need to pay for the full report?

Your short result is free. The optional extended report is a one-time $5.99 purchase.

How long does the scan take?

There are 10 questions and they usually take just a few minutes.

Is my data safe?

The test answers are stored in your browser. Stripe handles payments. Please review the Privacy Policy.

Can I use this more than once?

Yes. Each attempt reflects the answers you give at that time.

Who is this tool for?

For adults who want to reflect on communication, closeness, behaviour and trust.

What should I do after seeing my result?

Consider the issues you noticed and discuss them where appropriate. Seek qualified support if you feel unsafe or in distress.
